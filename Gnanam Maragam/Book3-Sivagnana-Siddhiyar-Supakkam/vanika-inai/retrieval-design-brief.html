Design note · for discussion

Retrieval you can defend

Enterprise search finds passages. Decisions need facts with an owner. The gap between the two is not model quality — it is a missing process. This note sketches that process, and where the idea came from.

6 min read Status: early thinking Applies to: Energy · Agriculture · Water
01

The problem is not search

Ask a retrieval system a question today and you get paragraphs. Nobody can say whether the answer is right, who stands behind it, or what it replaced.

Three specific failures show up in almost every programme, and they are structural rather than technical:

Extraction and interpretation are fused

The moment a document is parsed, it is also labelled. When the schema changes — and it always does — nothing can be re-derived without re-ingesting the world.

One confidence score, not two checks

"How sure are you?" is not one question. Disagreement between sources and outright error are different failures, and a single number averages them into false comfort.

Approval is a checkbox

Someone clicks approve. No name, no scope, no record of what they were entitled to sign. It looks like governance and is not.

02

The shape of the answer

Five things must be present for a claim to exist at all, two gates must pass, and one person must sign.

01AskThe requestwho wants it, for what decision, to what tolerance
02LightThe reasoning engineit can represent anything — it illuminates, it does not decide
03GatesIntakedocuments, images, sensors, feeds, people
04MediumThe evidence recordthe raw, immutable record of what arrived
05FormThe registrythe ontology that says what a thing is
Gate one — Doubt · conflict checkSources disagree. Surface it, hold it open. Do not average.
Gate two — Falsification · stress testTry to break the claim: units, physical limits, time, cross-source.
Signature · attestationa named person, within their scope, with a timestamp — not a status field
Why five and not one Each step has its own failure mode, so each can be measured, fixed and audited separately. Merging them is what makes today's systems fast and unauditable.

What we would build

Authority matrixClaim type × roleA signature that means something
GovernorBudget, backpressure, cheap/expensive routingCost stays proportional to consequence

One inference capability, used by several of these under different constraints — not a separate model per role. That is cheaper, more consistent, and easier to certify.

04

One case, end to end

A transformer nameplate disagrees with the asset register. This happens constantly and is quietly resolved by whoever is closest. Here is the same five stages and two gates, with the actual values.

AskThe request
"What is the rated capacity of TR-114?" A crew is being scheduled against it this week. Tolerance: exact. Who may sign: the substation engineer — a vendor brochure is not acceptable.
LightThe reasoning engine
Reads all four artefacts and proposes candidate readings. It does not choose between them, and it has no authority to.
GatesIntake
Four doors open: a nameplate photo from the field app, a 2004 commissioning certificate, a SCADA peak-load export, an email about a 2019 bushing replacement.
MediumThe evidence record
All four stored unmodified, hashed, timestamped. OCR returns 31.5 MVA, 132/33 kV, Ynd11 with positions and confidences. The word "capacity" does not exist yet at this stage — nothing has been labelled.
FormThe registry
Binds the percepts to asset AST-4471: name TR-114, class power transformer, property 31.5 MVA / 132/33 kV, function step-down, identity the physical unit in the Perambalur yard.
Gate oneConflict check
The register says 25 MVA, last touched in 1998. Nameplate and certificate say 31.5 MVA. The conflict is raised and held open, with both provenance chains attached. Not averaged.
Gate twoStress test
Serials match, so the certificate belongs to this unit. Peak load of 22.4 MW at 0.8 power factor implies 28 MVA — fits 31.5, does not fit 25. The "wrong unit" hypothesis is rejected and kept on the record with its reason.
SignatureAttestation
The substation engineer confirms the 2004 replacement from her own knowledge and signs, with name, scope and timestamp. The fact supersedes the 25 MVA entry, which is retained. The next query answers in milliseconds, with the whole trail attached.

06

Where this came from

You should know the source, because it is unusual.

I have been studying a 12th-century Tamil text on how a mind comes to know a thing. In one verse it describes perception as five constituents that must all be present, followed by two gates — doubt and misidentification — before a thing can be said to be known at all.

"உயிரினோடு உணர்வு வாயில் ஒளி உருவாதி பற்றி…" — with the self, its light of knowing, the sense-gates, the medium and the object's form, all engaged together…

I am borrowing a description of a process, the way one might borrow a model from biology or from military doctrine. What transfers is the structure, not the doctrine — and the structure happens to be unusually precise about where knowledge is produced and where it can go wrong.

Two things I took from it that I would not have designed myself. First, that the un-named stage is real and must be preserved separately from the named one — most systems collapse them at ingest. Second, that speed hides structure: a thing feels known instantly only because five steps ran fast, and the value of rebuilding those steps is precisely that they become inspectable.

Where the analogy breaks — and it does The text's knower is a conscious self that experiences. Software has none. So the human signature is not a governance formality bolted on at the end; it is the only place in the system where anyone actually knows anything. The pipeline produces warrant — a defensible, traceable claim. It does not produce truth, and I would not sell it as such.

Early-stage exploration, shared for reaction rather than decision. The architecture referenced here is developed in a separate working note; this page is the business summary.
