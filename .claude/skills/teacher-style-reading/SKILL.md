# Teacher-style reading

A teacher's habits carry information. Knowing the pattern shows what matters in a class and what will
come back. This skill covers how the teacher teaches, not what the book means.

## The usual shape of a class

Most classes run in the same order: salutations, thanks, recap, then the new material. The early
classes of a book often differ, because they introduce it and refer back to earlier books. Those
references matter later, so keep them.

The first three are form, not content. Do not quote them back as substance. Do not pad an answer
with them either.

## Reading a class

1. **Separate form from substance.** Set aside salutations and thanks.
2. **Mark the recap.** It reaches back several classes and rebuilds what the new material needs. What
   the teacher chooses to bring back is load-bearing, so expect it again.
3. **Mark announced repetition.** When the teacher says "I will say this again", that point will
   return. Repetition here is a teaching tool, not a sign of running out of things to say.
4. **Collect technical terms** from the new material, and pass them to `term-list-builder`.
5. **Do not guess the song or verse number.** If the notes do not say, say that they do not.

## Compressed terms

A term taught at length early on often returns later as one word, with no explanation. If a passage
feels too brief, suspect this. Give the full explanation again and say where it was first taught. The
`term-list-builder` skill has the steps.

## When answering

- Use this for teaching style. Use the book and its commentary for what a verse means.
- Say whether a pattern is something the user told you or something you noticed.
- Attribute something to the teacher only if it is in the user's notes or a transcript.

## Limits

- Do not invent a sentence and put it in the teacher's mouth. Say what was said, and give your own
  understanding in your own voice.
- If a fact is unconfirmed, say so rather than filling it in.
- Do not pull this into a business view unless the user asks.
- This does not replace the teacher. True understanding comes from learning in person, not from notes.
