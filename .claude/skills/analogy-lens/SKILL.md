# Analogy lens

Traditional texts can hold rigorous methods. What can transfer is the structure, not the purpose. This
works only if the text is reported honestly first and the analogy is allowed to fail.

Run this only when asked. In an ordinary answer, at most offer it in one line at the end.

## Context

Ask the user what the analogy is for: their field, their role, and the decisions they face. Generic
"management talk" is not the target. Aim at the concrete things they work with.

Keep details about the user's firm out of public files and out of external tools.

## The four steps

**1. What the text says.** Give the position in 2–3 lines, with its location. Settle this first. If a
reading cannot stand on its own, do not build on it.

**2. The problem it solved.** What did this idea solve in its own field? Set aside the theology and
hold on to the mechanism. For example, a theory of valid knowledge answers "how do we know this is
true, and who may judge?"

**3. Where it shows up today.** Name the actual thing: a diligence checklist, a model-risk register, a
data-quality gate, a decision record. "This could be used in companies" is too vague to be useful.

**4. Where the analogy breaks.** Always state this. An analogy with no stated break is decoration.

## Output

- Order: text › problem › today's fit › tool › limit.
- If a tool is asked for, give one that can be used: a checklist, table or one-page framework.
- Put the content in the answer itself. A file can be added after it, not instead of it.
- Language: technical terms in English, reasoning and quotations in the user's language.

## Limits

- Do not say the text supports a modern practice. It does not. Say "the structure of this idea
  resembles that".
- Do not stretch a thin analogy. Say it is thin.
- This is a thinking framework, not investment or legal advice.
- Keep the source's humility. Its goal is liberation, not productivity. Say where a business use
  flattens the idea.
- Separate what the book says from the user's own reading, and credit each to its source.
- Where a quality cannot be measured, say it is a judgement. Do not turn it into a score.

## From seed to proposal

For Step 4, start from seeds the user has picked, not ones you choose. Keep each seed's break in the
proposal. Proposals stay private: keep them out of public repositories unless the user decides
otherwise for that specific one.
